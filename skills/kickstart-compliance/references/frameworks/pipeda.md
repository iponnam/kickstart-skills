## What it is
Canada's federal private-sector privacy law. Part 1 sets the fair information principles in Schedule 1 (based on the CSA Model Code), access rights, and Division 1.1 mandatory reporting, notification and record-keeping for breaches of security safeguards. The OPC investigates complaints and audits.

## Who it applies to
s.4(1): "every organization in respect of personal information that (a) the organization collects, uses or discloses in the course of commercial activities; or (b) is about an employee of, or an applicant for employment with, the organization ... in connection with the operation of a federal work, undertaking or business." s.4(2) excludes government institutions under the Privacy Act and purely personal/domestic use, among others. Provinces with substantially similar laws (e.g. Quebec, Alberta, BC; Ontario/NB/NL/NS for health custodians) displace Part 1 for intra-provincial activity (s.26(2)(b) orders, not re-verified here).

## Deadlines and numbers
| What | Value | Official reference |
|---|---|---|
| Breach report to OPC | "as soon as feasible after the organization determines that the breach has occurred" | s.10.1(2) |
| Notification to individuals | "as soon as feasible after the organization determines that the breach has occurred" | s.10.1(6) |
| Breach record retention | "for 24 months after the day on which the organization determines that the breach has occurred" | Regs s.6(1) |
| Access request response | "not later than thirty days after receipt of the request"; extension "for a maximum of thirty days" | s.8(3), 8(4)(a) |
| Offences (knowing breach of s.10.1 or 10.3(1), obstruction) | fine "not exceeding $10,000" (summary) or "not exceeding $100,000" (indictable) | s.28 |
| Federal Court application window | "within one year after the report or notification is sent" | s.14(2) |

## Assessment and certification
No certification. OPC investigates complaints (s.11-13), may audit (s.18), and may publish findings; Federal Court can order compliance and damages (s.14-16). Parliamentary review every five years (s.29).
