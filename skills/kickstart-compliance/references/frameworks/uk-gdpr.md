## What it is
The UK's general data protection regime. The UK GDPR is the EU GDPR as retained in UK law at the end of the Brexit transition period and amended since. It is read with the Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA 2018). The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA) made substantial amendments: recognised legitimate interests, new automated decision-making rules (Arts. 22A–22D), new time-limit rules (Art. 12A), a controller complaints duty (DPA s. 164A), new transfer rules, and replacement of the Information Commissioner by a corporate Information Commission.

## Who it applies to
- Art. 3(1): processing "in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or a processor in the United Kingdom, regardless of whether the processing takes place in the United Kingdom or not."
- Art. 3(2): controllers or processors not established in the UK processing personal data of data subjects "who are in the United Kingdom", where processing relates to offering goods or services to them or "the monitoring of their behaviour as far as their behaviour takes place within the United Kingdom".
- Data protection fee: every data controller pays an annual charge unless exempt (SI 2018/480).

## Assessment and certification
No mandatory certification. The Information Commission enforces through information, assessment, interview and enforcement notices and penalty notices (DPA 2018 Part 6; DUAA ss. 97–101). Approved codes of conduct and certification remain voluntary (UK GDPR Arts. 40–42).
